Snap 24 · visual directions

Three ways to make it modern and sleek — led by type.

The rules of the game don't change: same board, same cards, same flow. What changes is the typographic voice and the surface around it. Each direction is a complete, real screen — pick one (or mix), then I port it into the actual Bevy build and bundle the fonts.

Assumption I'm running with: “modern + sleek + its own style” means a dark surface, real poker cards, and a type system distinctive enough to be the game's signature. If you actually want a light/bright take, say so and I'll re-cut these.

Why it reads as “sleek”: everything is monospace and metric — the board sits on a faint grid, the target is bracketed [24], the timer ticks T–9s [RUN]. One electric-lime accent does all the signalling. A cold, precise, tool-like identity; the further-est from the current build.

How to choose

  • Pick a letter, or a hybrid (“B's gold hairlines + C's mono timer”).
  • All three stay dark + real poker cards + one accent — they differ in type voice, which is the fastest lever for “has its own style”.
  • I verified nothing external here — these are our own game's directions, no brand assets involved.

What happens next

  • I port the chosen direction into the Bevy app (colors, spacing, card faces, control styling).
  • Fonts get bundled as .ttf (all three are open-source, SIL OFL) so they render offline in the game.
  • Motion + the difficulty slider / reveal / timer work from ticket 10 carry over unchanged.
