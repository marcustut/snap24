My call: go A × C — but if it must be one, pick A.

Why not pure C: its advantages (mono alignment, readout feel) are all about the data layer, not the game's identity — and its cost is exactly the thing you asked for, “its own style”. Mono + grid + neon is the most recognisable AI/dev-tool aesthetic there is; it would make Snap 24 look like everything else.

Why not pure A: it's genuinely ownable, but proportional serif numerals fight you on fractions (11/8, 13/24) and column alignment — and fractions are core to this game.

The hybrid resolves the actual tension: Fraunces owns the brand layer (wordmark, target, card ranks — that's where “own style” lives), Space Mono owns the instrument layer (computed value tokens, timer, score, status, operator keys — that's where alignment and precision live). One rule to keep it coherent: cards and the target are the game; everything counted or measured is mono.

Quick gut-check if you'd rather not hybridise: look at the 13/24 token in each column above. If it bugs you that it wobbles in A, or that C feels like a dashboard, that's your answer.
